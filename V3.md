## Preamble

*The Manifesto for Agile Software Development has guided our industry for over twenty-five years, and its impact cannot be overstated.*

*But we are in a new era of software engineering, and the foundation the Agile Manifesto was built on has shifted. The pace of how we deliver software has accelerated beyond what that original document envisioned. AI and the tools available today have fundamentally changed what individuals and small teams can achieve — and what the work of engineering itself looks like.*

*Software engineering remains the act of turning what the business needs into software that runs. What has changed is everything required to do that well.*

*Engineers no longer write code; they direct its creation and validate its outcomes. The art of typing code is no longer the work, and without the tools, engineers cannot do the job the discipline now requires.*

*Code can now be produced with minimal effort; more of the work now lies in understanding the problem, making good decisions about what to build, and ensuring what ships actually delivers value. This has also increased the cognitive load on engineers and as tools take on execution, what remains sits higher up and demands more.*

*Vibe coding — accepting AI-generated code without doing the work to understand it — is becoming routine. That does not make it engineering. AI assists; it does not absolve. Code that ships without comprehension accrues cognitive debt — the gap between what runs and what anyone understands.*

*Some things have not changed. Tools have not replaced the team: the speed of change has accelerated, and constant communication is the only way alignment keeps up. And understanding what the customer actually needs is still the root — AI amplifies engineers who understand the problem; it exposes those who don't.*

*Grounded in what hasn't changed and shaped by what has, this is a manifesto for how software engineering works now.*

*— Allan Crain*

# The Software Engineering Manifesto

## Values

*Software engineering is the discipline that turns what the business needs into software that runs.*

We are uncovering better ways of engineering software by building it and helping others build it.

Here are our core values:

**Understanding the customer and the problem comes before everything** — not the brief, not the ticket, but what they actually need.

**Software is built through tools, directed by capable, motivated engineers.** Outcomes are determined by engineering judgement, amplified by tools.

**Feedback loops belong everywhere** — shorten and amplify them. They are how you find out you're wrong before it's expensive, and right before it's too late.

**What you ship, you own** — caring about how fast it goes out, how well it lands, and how securely and reliably it operates and endures.

---

## Principles

We follow these principles:

1. Our highest priority is a working product that delivers measurable value to the customer.

2. Continuous attention to technical excellence and sound architecture are critical. Weak standards are magnified by faster delivery and tools.

3. Deep understanding of the problem precedes every good solution. Working software — prototype or production — is how you best learn what is actually needed. Speed without understanding produces output, not engineering.

4. Build around motivated, capable individuals directing tools that compound their judgement and amplify their capability — not bypassing them. Trust them to define and deliver the work.

5. Roles converge around the customer problem, not around artefacts. Everyone shaping a product should be deep in their discipline, capable across the others, and working the problem together, not in sequence.

6. Clear communication and strong, constant collaboration are how engineering scales beyond individuals. Where collaboration is weak, delivery slows and quality corrupts.

7. Welcome change driven by learned evidence. Resist change driven by anything else. When evidence disagrees with the plan, the plan loses.

8. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then commits to actions and follows through.

9. Deliver and deploy continuously in small increments, but manage the rate of change that customers experience.

10. Engineers are ultimately accountable for what they ship — regardless of how it is produced. Accountability for the software cannot be delegated to the tools used to build it.

11. Continuous automated verification, validation and deliberate human review are as important as the code. Every part of the system needs automated tests; no discipline is the exception.

12. Documentation and specifications are operational infrastructure, not overhead — they need to live alongside the code they describe and direct, and be readable by both the humans and the tools that work on them.

13. Systems where data drives behaviour are non-deterministic by nature, and only as good as the data they learn from, retrieve from, and act on. Bad data produces bad outcomes.

14. Simplicity — the art of maximising the amount of work not done — is essential. Remove unessential complexity from requirements, software, and team processes.

15. Work needs to be done at a sustainable pace that protects both the people and their ability to deliver, indefinitely.
